Proposition A.15. ([Lurie 2009, Propositions 5.2.7.12 and 5.5.4.15])

Let \(C\) be a presentable category and let \(\Sigma\) be a strongly saturated class of small generation.

  1. The inclusion \(\Loc_{\Sigma}(C) \hookrightarrow C\) of the \(\Sigma\)-local objects in \(C\) admits an accessible left adjoint \(L\colon C \to \Loc_{\Sigma}(C)\).

  2. The category \(\Loc_{\Sigma}(C)\) is presentable.

  3. We have \(\ker(L) = \Sigma\), i.e. a morphism in \(C\) is inverted by \(L\) if and only if it lies in \(\Sigma\).

  4. For every category \(E\), precomposition with \(L\) induces a fully faithful functor

    \[\Fun(\Loc_{\Sigma}(C),E)\longrightarrow\Fun(C,E)\]

    whose essential image consists of the functors that invert every morphism in \(\Sigma\).

Proof
Let \(S\) be a small set generating \(\Sigma\) under strong saturation. For each object \(Z\), the arrows \(a\) for which \(\Hom_C(a,Z)\) is an isomorphism form a strongly saturated class. Thus \(S\) and \(\Sigma\) have the same local objects. By [SHT, Theorem 23.2.2], part (4), these form a presentable reflective subcategory. The reflector, being a left adjoint, preserves colimits.
Apply Proposition A.11 to the ordinary saturation \(S^s\). Factoring \(X\to *\) gives \(X\to X'\to *\) with the first map in \(S^s\) and \(X'\) local. For local \(Z\), mapping the first map into \(Z\) gives an isomorphism. It is therefore the unit \(X\to LX\), and belongs to \(\Sigma\). The kernel of \(L\) contains \(S\) and is strongly saturated, so contains \(\Sigma\). Conversely, if \(Lf\) is invertible, naturality of the unit and 2-out-of-3 give \(f\in\Sigma\). Finally the unit identifies every functor inverting \(\Sigma\) with its restriction to the local objects composed with \(L\). The counit identifies the other composite with the identity, also on natural transformations.
