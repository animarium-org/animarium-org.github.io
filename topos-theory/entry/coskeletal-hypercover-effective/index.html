Lemma 6.48. ([Lurie 2009, Lemma 6.5.3.9])

Let \(X\) be an object of a topos \(T\). If \(U_\bullet\to X\) is an augmented hypercover which is \(n\)-coskeletal in \(T_{/X}\), then its augmentation \(\abs{U_\bullet}\to X\) is an isomorphism.

Proof
Work in the slice over \(X\), and write \(U=U_\bullet\). We may compute a simplicial realization using only injective maps in \(\simp\). For each \([m]\), the comma category in the finality criterion is the opposite of the category of strings in \([m]\) and face inclusions. Appending \(m\) gives an endofunctor receiving natural maps from the identity and from the constant string \((m)\). Thus its nerve is contractible. Moreover the semisimplicial restriction of an \(n\)-coskeleton is still an \(n\)-coskeleton. In degree \(k\) the right Kan extension is computed on the poset of faces of \([k]\) of dimension at most \(n\): factoring each simplex through its image exhibits the inclusion of faces as final in the indexing category before passing to opposites.
For \(n=0\) it is the Čech nerve of the effective epimorphism \(U_0\to *\). For \(n>0\) put \(V=\cosk_{n-1}U\). This is again a hypercover, with invertible matching maps from degree \(n\) onwards. Inductively \(\abs V\simeq *\). The map \(U_k\to V_k\) is an isomorphism for \(k<n\). For \(k\geq n\), extending boundary data on the \((n-1)\)-skeleton of \([k]\) to its \(n\)-skeleton amounts to choosing, for each nondegenerate \(n\)-face, a lift along \(U_n\to V_n\) of its boundary. Distinct \(n\)-faces meet in lower dimensions. The resulting map is therefore a finite composite of base changes of this effective epimorphism. Higher degrees are determined by coskeletality.
Form the bisimplicial object \(W_{p,q}=U_q^{\times_{V_q}(p+1)}\). Its realization in the first variable is \(V_q\) by effectiveness. The diagonal \(D_k=W_{k,k}\) thus realizes to \(\abs V\), since its diagonal is final by [SCT, Proposition 10.4.6] and colimits commute. Repeating each entry gives a simplicial map \(U\to D\). We construct a retraction after forgetting degeneracies.
Below degree \(n\), both semisimplicial diagrams identify with \(V\) and the map is the identity. In degree \(n\) they map to the same matching object \(V_n\). To extend a map on lower degrees to degree \(n\) is exactly to give a map over this matching object: the only nonidentity arrows out of degree \(n\) are its faces and their composites. In that slice the map is the diagonal \(U_n\to U_n^{\times_{V_n}(n+1)}\). Projection onto the first factor retracts it. Right Kan extension into the \(n\)-coskeletal target \(U\) now gives a map \(D\to U\) of semisimplicial objects whose composite on \(U\) is the identity, since it is the identity through degree \(n\). Consequently \(\abs U\) is a retract of the terminal object \(\abs D\) and is terminal.
