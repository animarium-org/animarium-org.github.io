Proposition 5.35.

The following conditions are equivalent for a class of morphisms \(K\):

  1. \(K\) is a congruence;

  2. \(K\) is strongly saturated and stable under base change;

  3. \(K\) is strongly saturated and closed under finite limits;

  4. \(K\) is saturated and closed under finite limits;

  5. \(K\) is an acyclic class and is closed under diagonals: if \(f\colon X \to Y\) is in \(K\), then so is \(\Delta_f\colon X \to X \times_Y X\).

Proof
The equivalence between (1) and (2) is the definition. For (2)\(\Rightarrow\)(3), the diagonal of a map in \(K\) is a section of a base change of that map and hence belongs to \(K\). For a map of cospans with components \(X_i\to Y_i\) in \(K\), the map of pullbacks factors through \(X_1\times_{Y_0}X_2\), where \(0\) denotes the common target. The first factor is a base change of the diagonal of \(X_0\to Y_0\); the remaining factors replace \(X_1\) and \(X_2\) in succession and are base changes of their component maps. This proves pullback closure and the terminal arrow is an identity. Clearly (3) implies (4). Conversely, finite-limit closure implies stability under base change, since a base-change square is a pullback in \(\Ar(T)\). Moreover, Lemma 5.34 gives left cancellation. Saturation already gives composition and right cancellation, so \(K\) satisfies 2-out-of-3. This proves (4)\(\Rightarrow\)(2).
Condition (2) implies (5): the projection \(X\times_YX\to X\) is a base change of \(f\), and its section \(\Delta_f\) belongs to \(K\) by 2-out-of-3. Conversely, assume (5). Since \(K\) is saturated, it is closed under composition and has right cancellation. It remains to check left cancellation. Given \(X \xrightarrow{f} Y \xrightarrow{g} Z\) with \(g, gf \in K\), factor \(f\) as
\[X \xrightarrow{(\id, f)} X \times_Z Y \xrightarrow{\pr_Y} Y.\]
The second map is a base change of \(gf \in K\), hence in \(K\). The first is a base change of \(\Delta_g\), which is in \(K\) by assumption. Thus \(f \in K\).
