Proposition 4.32.

The inclusion \(\Topos\hookrightarrow\Cat\), \(\varphi\mapsto\varphi_*\), preserves limits indexed by \(I\catop\) for every small filtered category \(I\).

Proof
We follow the cartesian-section construction of [Lurie 2009, Section 6.3.3]. Write the diagram as \(I\catop\to\Topos\). We may replace \(I\) by a final directed poset, by [Lurie 2009, Proposition 5.3.1.18], since its opposite is initial for both limits. For \(i\le j\), write \(r_{ij}\colon T_j\to T_i\) for direct image and \(l_{ij}\) for its left adjoint. Let \(p\) be the cartesian fibration over \(I\) with these pullback functors. It is also cocartesian with transport \(l_{ij}\). Write \(H\) for its category of sections and \(D\) for its full subcategory of cartesian sections. Straightening identifies \(D\) with \(\lim_{I\catop}T_i\).
We first show that \(H\) is a logos. Its colimits and finite limits are pointwise by Proposition B.1, so the Giraud axioms other than presentability can be checked in the fibers. For presentability, express the base as the colimit of its simplices and take the corresponding limit of section categories. Over an edge \(i\to j\) the category of sections is \(T_i\times_{T_j}\Ar(T_j)\), using \(l_{ij}\) and source evaluation. Over a string of edges it is the iterated pullback of these categories over their common fibers, by composition in a category. All the functors in the pullbacks and all restriction functors preserve colimits. Thus [SHT, Theorem 23.2.2] and [SHT, Theorem 23.2.3] give presentability over each simplex and then over \(I\).
For \(j\in I\) let \(I_j=\{k\mid k\geq j\}\). Restriction of sections to \(I_j\) has fully faithful right adjoint given at \(i\) by the limit of \(r_{ik}X_k\) over \(k\geq i,j\), with arrows induced by the section. This is the right Kan extension formula, since maps into these limits are precisely compatible cones. Limits here are indexed in the forward direction. This reflector onto the right Kan extensions preserves finite limits by pointwise computation. It is accessible: restriction preserves colimits and its right adjoint, between presentable categories, is accessible. A cartesian section is a right Kan extension from every \(I_j\), since the diagram in the formula is then constant at \(X_i\) and the directed indexing poset is weakly contractible. Conversely, for \(i\leq j\) the poset \(k\geq i,j\) has initial object \(j\), so right extension from \(I_j\) identifies \(X_i\to r_{ij}X_j\) as an isomorphism. Thus Proposition A.16 exhibits \(D\) as an accessible left exact localization of \(H\), and Corollary 2.17 makes \(D\) a logos.
Evaluation \(e_i\colon D\to T_i\) has a left exact left adjoint. Indeed restriction induces an equivalence on cartesian sections over \(I\) and \(I_i\) by finality of \(I_i\to I\). Over \(I_i\), first send \(A\in T_i\) to the section \(j\mapsto l_{ij}A\), then reflect into cartesian sections by the preceding construction for \(I_i\). The first functor is left adjoint to evaluation on all sections by cocartesian transport from the initial object \(i\). Both steps preserve finite limits.
Finally let \(h\colon S\to D\) have components \(h_i=e_i h\) which are morphisms of topoi, with left adjoints \(a_i\). Its left adjoint is
\[X\longmapsto\colim_{i\in I} a_i X_i .\]
The arrows are induced by \(l_{ij}X_i\to X_j\) and \(a_jl_{ij}\simeq a_i\). Mapping the colimit into \(Y\in S\) gives the limit over \(I\catop\) of \(\Hom_{T_i}(X_i,h_iY)\), which is \(\Hom_D(X,hY)\) by the description of \(D\) as a limit. Each \(a_ie_i\) preserves finite limits and filtered colimits in \(S\) commute with finite limits, so the left adjoint is left exact. Conversely every morphism of topoi into \(D\) has geometric components by the assertion about evaluation. This proves the universal property in \(\Topos\).
