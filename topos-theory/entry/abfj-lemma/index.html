Proposition 5.37. ([Anel et al. 2024, Section 2.2.7])

If \(L\) is an acyclic class, then \(D(L)\) is again an acyclic class. If \(L\) is of small generation, then so is \(D(L)\); in this case both classes are left classes of modalities.

Proof
Stability under base change and the inclusion of all isomorphisms are immediate. For composition, let \(A \xrightarrow{f} B \xrightarrow{g} C\) lie in \(D(L)\). Then \(gf\in L\), and \(\Delta_{gf}\) factors as
\[A \xrightarrow{ \Delta_f } A \times_B A \longrightarrow A \times_C A ,\]
where the second map is a base change of \(\Delta_g\). Since \(\Delta_f,\Delta_g\in L\) and \(L\) is an acyclic class, \(\Delta_{gf}\in L\).
For colimits, let \(f_{\bullet}\colon X_{\bullet}\to Y_{\bullet}\) be a diagram in \(\Ar(T)\) with every \(f_i\in D(L)\), and write \(f\colon X\to Y\) for its colimit. We already know \(f\in L\). For this small diagram we may work in the smaller acyclic class generated by all \(f_i\) and \(\Delta_{f_i}\). It is contained in \(L\) and defines a modality by Proposition 5.32. The following argument applied to that modality proves that \(\Delta_f\) belongs to \(L\). We can therefore use modal descent from Proposition 5.23.
For a morphism \(i\to j\) in the indexing category, the gap map of the naturality square is
\[X_i\longrightarrow Y_i\times_{Y_j}X_j.\]
It factors as
\[X_i\longrightarrow X_i\times_{Y_j}X_j \longrightarrow Y_i\times_{Y_j}X_j.\]
The first map is a base change of \(\Delta_{f_j}\), and the second is a base change of \(f_i\). Both lie in \(L\), so the transformation \(f_{\bullet}\) is \(L\)-cartesian. Acyclic descent now shows that for every \(i\) the square is \(L\)-cartesian. Equivalently, the gap map \(h_i\colon X_i\to Y_i\times_YX\) lies in \(L\).
By universality of colimits, the diagonal \(\Delta_f\) is the colimit of the maps \(X_i\to X_i\times_YX\). Each of these factors as
\[X_i\xrightarrow{\Delta_{f_i}}X_i\times_{Y_i}X_i \longrightarrow X_i\times_YX,\]
where the second map is a base change of \(h_i\). Thus both maps lie in \(L\). Closure under colimits gives \(\Delta_f\in L\), proving that \(D(L)\) is saturated. For the size assertion, take a small generating set for \(L\). The construction in Proposition A.11 gives an accessible right-factor functor on \(\Ar(T)\): attach cells functorially by taking the coproduct parametrized by the whole anima of squares at each stage. These animae commute with sufficiently filtered colimits by compactness of the cell domains and codomains, and colimits of stages preserve that property. An arrow lies in \(D(L)\) precisely when the right factors of it and its diagonal are isomorphisms. The functor taking these two right factors is accessible, since diagonals commute with filtered colimits in a logos. Pulling back the full subcategory of pairs of isomorphisms therefore exhibits \(D(L)\) as accessible by [Lurie 2009, Proposition 5.4.7.3]. Since its colimits are computed in \(\Ar(T)\), a small set of accessible generators generates it under saturation.
