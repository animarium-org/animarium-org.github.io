Proposition A.11. ([Lurie 2009, Proposition 5.5.5.7])

Let \(C\) be a presentable category and let \(L\) be a saturated class of small generation. Then the pair \((L,L^{\perp})\) forms a factorization system on \(C\).

Proof
Write \(L=S^s\) for a set \(S\). For \(u\colon A\to B\) in \(S\) and \(m\geq 0\), form the map
\[(B\otimes\partial\Delta^m)\amalg_{A\otimes\partial\Delta^m}(A\otimes\Delta^m) \longrightarrow B\otimes\Delta^m .\]
Here tensoring uses the anima of the indicated simplicial set, with empty boundary for \(m=0\). These maps belong to \(L\): the first is \(u\), and each successive one is the fold of the preceding map, by decomposing a sphere as two disks along their boundary. For any \(v\colon P\to Q\) its fold \(Q\amalg_P Q\to Q\) is the pushout in \(\Ar(C)\) of \(\id_Q\leftarrow v\to\id_Q\). Denote the resulting set by \(S'\).
Factor \(X\to Y\) as follows. Start at \(X_0=X\). At each successor stage attach a copy of the codomain of \(v\) along its domain for every square from \(v\in S'\) to \(X_\alpha\to Y\), choosing one square in each component of the anima of squares. Take colimits at limit ordinals. Choose an infinite regular cardinal \(\lambda\) for which the domains of \(S'\) are \(\lambda\)-compact. Every square against \(X_\lambda\to Y\) comes from some stage, since the anima of such squares is a pullback of Hom animae and filtered colimits commute with finite limits in \(\An\). Hence it admits a filler. For a fixed square against \(u\in S\), the tests for \(m=0,1,\ldots\) say exactly that its anima of fillers is nonempty and every map from a sphere into it extends over the disk. This follows by tensor-Hom adjunction applied to the displayed map. Thus the filler anima is contractible and \(X_\lambda\to Y\) lies in \(S^\perp=L^\perp\), the equality following from [SHT, Proposition 19.1.5].
The map \(X\to X_\lambda\) belongs to \(L\): successor maps are cobase changes of coproducts of maps of \(S'\), and at a limit stage the map from \(X\) is the colimit of the preceding maps from \(X\). This gives the required factorization. Orthogonality gives uniqueness and hence a factorization system.
