A.3. Strongly saturated classes

When studying localizations of presentable categories, we require a stronger stability property.

Definition A.12.

Let \(L\) be a class of morphisms of a presentable category \(C\). We say \(L\) is strongly saturated if it is saturated and closed under 2-out-of-3.

As with saturated classes, we say a strongly saturated class \(L\) is of small generation if it is the smallest strongly saturated class containing some set of morphisms \(\Sigma\). The following two results identify these classes with kernels of accessible Bousfield localizations.

Definition A.13.

Let \(\varphi\colon C \to D\) be a cocontinuous functor between presentable categories. We define its kernel as the collection of morphisms in \(C\) that is inverted by \(\varphi\):

\[\ker(\varphi) \quad := \quad \{f\colon X \to Y \in \Ar(C) \mid \varphi(f)\text{ is an isomorphism}\}.\]

Proposition A.14. ([Lurie 2009, Proposition 5.5.4.16])

Let \(\varphi\colon C \to D\) be a cocontinuous functor between presentable categories. Then \(\ker(\varphi)\) is strongly saturated and of small generation.

Proof
The class contains the isomorphisms and satisfies 2-out-of-3. Its full subcategory in \(\Ar(C)\) is the pullback of \(\Ar(C)\to\Ar(D)\) along the identity-arrow functor \(D\to\Ar(D)\), since the latter identifies \(D\) with the full subcategory of isomorphisms. Both functors preserve colimits, and the arrow categories are presentable by [SHT, Theorem 23.2.2]. By [SHT, Theorem 23.2.3] this pullback is presentable and its inclusion preserves colimits. Thus the kernel is closed under colimits of arrows, and any small set of colimit generators of the pullback generates the kernel as a strongly saturated class.

Proposition A.15. ([Lurie 2009, Propositions 5.2.7.12 and 5.5.4.15])

Let \(C\) be a presentable category and let \(\Sigma\) be a strongly saturated class of small generation.

  1. The inclusion \(\Loc_{\Sigma}(C) \hookrightarrow C\) of the \(\Sigma\)-local objects in \(C\) admits an accessible left adjoint \(L\colon C \to \Loc_{\Sigma}(C)\).

  2. The category \(\Loc_{\Sigma}(C)\) is presentable.

  3. We have \(\ker(L) = \Sigma\), i.e. a morphism in \(C\) is inverted by \(L\) if and only if it lies in \(\Sigma\).

  4. For every category \(E\), precomposition with \(L\) induces a fully faithful functor

    \[\Fun(\Loc_{\Sigma}(C),E)\longrightarrow\Fun(C,E)\]

    whose essential image consists of the functors that invert every morphism in \(\Sigma\).

Proof
Let \(S\) be a small set generating \(\Sigma\) under strong saturation. For each object \(Z\), the arrows \(a\) for which \(\Hom_C(a,Z)\) is an isomorphism form a strongly saturated class. Thus \(S\) and \(\Sigma\) have the same local objects. By [SHT, Theorem 23.2.2], part (4), these form a presentable reflective subcategory. The reflector, being a left adjoint, preserves colimits.
Apply Proposition A.11 to the ordinary saturation \(S^s\). Factoring \(X\to *\) gives \(X\to X'\to *\) with the first map in \(S^s\) and \(X'\) local. For local \(Z\), mapping the first map into \(Z\) gives an isomorphism. It is therefore the unit \(X\to LX\), and belongs to \(\Sigma\). The kernel of \(L\) contains \(S\) and is strongly saturated, so contains \(\Sigma\). Conversely, if \(Lf\) is invertible, naturality of the unit and 2-out-of-3 give \(f\in\Sigma\). Finally the unit identifies every functor inverting \(\Sigma\) with its restriction to the local objects composed with \(L\). The counit identifies the other composite with the identity, also on natural transformations.

Proposition A.16.

In a logos \(T\), the intersection of a small family of accessible left exact reflective subcategories is again an accessible left exact reflective subcategory.

Proof
Write \(P_a\) for the corresponding endofunctors with their units. Choose an infinite regular \(\kappa\) such that all \(P_a\) preserve \(\kappa\)-filtered colimits. The empty intersection is \(T\). Otherwise well-order the family and apply its reflectors in that order, repeating the entire list \(\kappa\) times. At each limit ordinal take the colimit of the preceding stages. This defines a functor \(X\mapsto PX\) with a unit \(X\to PX\). For each \(a\) the stages immediately following an application of \(P_a\) form a cofinal \(\kappa\)-filtered chain. Their colimit is \(P_a\)-local since \(P_a\) preserves it. For \(Z\) local for all \(a\), mapping the chain into \(Z\) gives a diagram in which every stage maps isomorphically to \(\Hom_T(X,Z)\), by the adjunctions and the colimit formula at limit stages. Thus \(P\) reflects into the intersection. Each stage preserves finite limits: this holds at successor stages by composition and at limit stages because filtered colimits commute with finite limits in a logos (apply a left exact presheaf presentation and [SCT, Theorem 16.2.9]). Each stage preserves \(\kappa\)-filtered colimits as well, by commutation of colimits. Hence \(P\) is accessible and left exact. The intersection is presentable by Proposition A.15, since its local objects are specified by the union of the small generating sets of the kernels, supplied by Proposition A.14.
