Dualizable categories

← Previous chapterChapter 2 → Next chapterChapter 4 ← Previous sectionSection 3.1 → Next sectionSection 3.3

We now specialize to presentable stable categories. Recall from Section A.5 the tensor product on \(\PrL \), and that compactly generated categories are dualizable in \(\PrL _{\st }\) by Corollary A.32.

Definition 3.11. A dualizable category is a dualizable object of \(\PrL _{\st }\). We denote by \(\Pr ^{\dual } \subseteq \PrL _{\st }\) the subcategory whose objects are the dualizable categories and whose morphisms are the internal left adjoints, i.e. those functors in \(\PrL _{\st }\) whose right adjoint also lies in \(\PrL _{\st }\), i.e. preserves colimits.

Explicitly, a presentable stable category \(C\) is dualizable if there exist a presentable stable category \(C^{\vee }\) and colimit-preserving functors \[ \ev \colon C \otimes C^{\vee } \to \Sp , \qquad \coev \colon \Sp \to C^{\vee } \otimes C, \] satisfying the triangle identities: the composites \[ C \xrightarrow {C \otimes \coev } C \otimes C^{\vee } \otimes C \xrightarrow {\ev \otimes C} C \qquad \text {and}\qquad C^{\vee } \xrightarrow {\coev \otimes C^{\vee }} C^{\vee } \otimes C \otimes C^{\vee } \xrightarrow {C^{\vee } \otimes \ev } C^{\vee } \] are equivalent to the identity. Since \(\PrL _{\st }\) is closed, the dual is then necessarily given by \[ C^{\vee } \simeq \Fun ^{\colim }(C,\Sp ), \] the internal hom from \(C\) to the unit, with \(\ev \) the evaluation functor.

Example 3.12. Let \(A\) be an \(\bbE _1\)-ring and let \(\Mod _A\) denote the category of right \(A\)-modules, so that \(\Mod _A \simeq \Ind (\Perf _A)\). Then \(\Mod _A\) is dualizable with dual \(\Mod _{A\catop }\), the category of left \(A\)-modules. The evaluation and coevaluation are given by \[ \ev \colon \Mod _A \otimes \Mod _{A\catop } \to \Sp , \quad (M,N) \mapsto M \otimes _A N, \qquad \coev \colon \Sp \to \Mod _{A\catop } \otimes \Mod _A \simeq \mathrm {BMod}_{A,A}, \quad \S \mapsto A, \] where the coevaluation sends the sphere spectrum to \(A\) regarded as an \(A\)-bimodule. This is a special case of Corollary A.32, using the equivalence \(\Perf _A\catop \simeq \Perf _{A\catop }\), \(P \mapsto \hom _A(P,A)\).

Remark 3.13. Note that a right adjoint of a functor between stable categories automatically preserves finite colimits, so it preserves all colimits if and only if it preserves filtered colimits. So the morphisms in \(\Pr ^{\dual }\) are precisely the strongly continuous functors.

Remark 3.14. By Corollary A.32, every compactly generated category \(C = \Ind (C_0)\) is dualizable. Moreover, every colimit-preserving functor preserving compact objects is strongly continuous, so a morphism in \(\Pr ^{\dual }\) by Remark 3.13. So we get a fully faithful inclusion \[ \PrL _{\omega ,\st } \subseteq \Pr ^{\dual }. \]

Remark 3.15. Dualizable objects of a symmetric monoidal category are closed under tensor products, and the tensor product of two strongly continuous functors is again strongly continuous: since \(- \otimes D\) is a functor of \((\infty ,2)\)-categories, it preserves adjunctions, so the right adjoint of \(f \otimes D\) is \(f^R \otimes D\), which again preserves colimits. Hence the symmetric monoidal structure on \(\PrL _{\st }\) restricts to \(\Pr ^{\dual }\). Moreover, the inclusion \(\Pr ^{\dual } \hookrightarrow \PrL _{\st }\) preserves colimits (Proposition 7.2), and \(\Pr ^{\dual }\) admits all limits, although these are not computed in \(\PrL _{\st }\); see Section 7.4 for finite limits.

The following theorem is the main result of this chapter: it shows that dualizable categories are precisely the compactly assembled presentable stable categories.

Theorem 3.16 (Lurie, [Lurie (2018), Proposition D.7.3.1]). Let \(C \in \PrL _{\st }\). Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\(C\) is dualizable;

(2)

\(C\) is compactly assembled;

(3)

There exists a localization \(D \to E\) in \(\PrL _{\omega ,\st }\) such that \(C = \ker (D \to E)\);

(4)

\(C\) has the left lifting property with respect to any localization in \(\PrL _{\st }\): for every localization \(D \to E\) and any \(C \to E\), there exists a lift \(C \to D\):

The proof first connects compact assembly, dualizability and lifting through localizations. The remaining condition gives a concrete presentation: a compactly assembled category embeds strongly continuously in a compactly generated category with compactly generated quotient.

Proof.

(2) \(\implies \) (1): Every compactly assembled category is a retract of a compactly generated one. Remark 3.14 shows that compactly generated categories are dualizable, and since dualizable objects are closed under retracts we are done.

(1) \(\implies \) (4): Assume \(C\) is dualizable, and consider a cofiber sequence \[ K \hookrightarrow D \to E \] in \(\PrL _{\st }\). Tensoring with \(C^{\vee }\) preserves colimits, so gives another cofiber sequence \[ C^{\vee } \otimes K \to C^{\vee } \otimes D \to C^{\vee } \otimes E. \] In particular, the functor \(C^{\vee } \otimes D \to C^{\vee } \otimes E\) is again a localization, hence essentially surjective. It follows that the functor \(\Sp \to C^{\vee } \otimes E\) classifying \(C \to E\) lifts to a functor \(\Sp \to C^{\vee } \otimes D\) classifying a lift \(C \to D\).

(4) \(\implies \) (2): Let \(C_0 \subseteq C\) be a small dense stable subcategory, and consider the adjunction \[ y\colon C \rightleftarrows \Ind (C_0) \noloc \colim . \] Since \(y\) is fully faithful, it follows that \(\colim \colon \Ind (C_0) \to C\) is a localization, and so by (4) this admits a section in \(\PrL _{\st }\). It follows that \(C\) is a retract of \(\Ind (C_0)\), hence it is compactly assembled.

(3) \(\implies \) (2): Consider a fiber sequence \[ C \hookrightarrow D \to E. \] Since \(D \to E\) preserves compact objects, its right adjoint preserves colimits. By Lemma 2.13, the kernel inclusion \(C \to D\) also has a colimit-preserving right adjoint \(D \to C\). This right adjoint is a retraction, so \(C\) is a retract of \(D\) in \(\PrL _{\st }\).

(2) \(\implies \) (3): As before, we may find some \(\lambda \) such that \(\colim \colon \Ind (C^{\lambda }) \to C\) admits a left adjoint \(C \to \Ind (C^{\lambda })\). Then consider the cofiber sequence \[ C \xhookrightarrow {\hat {y}} \Ind (C^{\lambda }) \to \cofib (\hat {y}) \simeq \fib (\colim ). \] The localization functor \(\Ind (C^{\lambda }) \to \cofib (\hat {y})\) admits a right adjoint in \(\PrL _{\st }\), and so it preserves compact objects. It in particular follows that \(\cofib (\hat {y})\) is compactly generated, i.e. \(\cofib (\hat {y}) \simeq \Ind (\Calk ^{\cont }_{\lambda }(C))\) where \(\Calk ^{\cont }_{\lambda }(C) := \cofib (\hat {y})^{\omega }\) is the continuous \(\lambda \)-Calkin category. So this gives (3). □

There are several further useful characterizations. Recall that a presentable category \(C\) is \(\kappa \)-compactly generated if \(\Ind _{\kappa }(C^{\kappa }) \simeq C\).

Proposition 3.17. Let \(C \in \PrL _{\st }\). Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\(C\) is dualizable;

(2)

The functor \(\colim \colon \Ind (C) \to C\) preserves small products;

(3)

For every regular cardinal \(\kappa \) such that \(C\) is \(\kappa \)-compactly generated, the functor \(\colim \colon \Ind (C^{\kappa }) \to C\) admits a left adjoint;

(4)

There exist a regular cardinal \(\kappa \) such that \(C\) is \(\kappa \)-compactly generated and the functor \(\colim \colon \Ind (C^{\kappa }) \to C\) admits a left adjoint;

(5)

There exists a fully faithful strongly continuous functor \(C \hookrightarrow D\) into a compactly generated category \(D\).

Condition (2) is one formulation of Grothendieck’s axiom (AB6), see Chapter 4.

Proof.

(1) \(\implies \) (2): By Theorem 3.16, \(\colim \) admits a left adjoint \(\hat {y}\), hence preserves all limits.

(2) \(\implies \) (3): Let \(\kappa \) be such that \(C\) is \(\kappa \)-compactly generated, let \(j\colon C^{\kappa } \hookrightarrow C\) be the inclusion and \(F = \Ind (j)\colon \Ind (C^{\kappa }) \hookrightarrow \Ind (C)\). The functor \(F\) is fully faithful. Its right adjoint is restriction of presheaves along \(j\): for \(X \in \Ind (C)\), the presheaf \(d \mapsto \Map _{\Ind (C)}(y(d),X)\) on \(C^{\kappa }\) preserves finite limits, so belongs to \(\Ind (C^{\kappa })\). Here we use the description of the Ind-completion of a category with finite colimits as finite-limit-preserving presheaves. This constructs \(F^R\) without a presentability assumption on the large category \(\Ind (C)\). Since representables are compact, this restriction functor preserves filtered colimits. We claim that \[ \colim _C \simeq \colim _{\kappa } \circ F^R, \] where \(\colim _{\kappa }\colon \Ind (C^{\kappa }) \to C\) denotes the colimit functor. Both sides preserve filtered colimits, so it suffices to compare them on objects \(y(c)\) with \(c \in C\), naturally in \(c\). For \(d \in C^{\kappa }\) we have \(\Map (y(d),F^Ry(c)) \simeq \Map _C(d,c)\), so \(F^Ry(c)\) is the ind-object \(\quotcolim _{(d \to c) \in (C^{\kappa })_{/c}} d\); note that \((C^{\kappa })_{/c}\) is \(\kappa \)-filtered with colimit \(c\), as \(C \simeq \Ind _{\kappa }(C^{\kappa })\). Hence \(\colim _{\kappa }F^Ry(c) \simeq c \simeq \colim _C y(c)\), naturally in \(c\).

Now let \((X_s)_{s \in S}\) be a family of objects of \(\Ind (C^{\kappa })\). Since \(F^R\) preserves products and \(F^RF \simeq \id \), we have \(\prod _s X_s \simeq F^R(\prod _s FX_s)\), and hence \[ \colim _{\kappa }\Big (\prod _s X_s\Big ) \simeq \colim _C\Big (\prod _s FX_s\Big ) \simeq \prod _s \colim _C(FX_s) \simeq \prod _s \colim _{\kappa }(X_s), \] using (2). So \(\colim _{\kappa }\) preserves products. It is moreover an exact functor between stable categories, so it preserves finite limits, and hence all small limits. Since it also preserves filtered colimits, it admits a left adjoint by the adjoint functor theorem (Theorem A.19).

(3) \(\implies \) (4): Every presentable category is \(\kappa \)-compactly generated for some \(\kappa \).

(4) \(\implies \) (5): Let \(\hat {y}\colon C \to \Ind (C^{\kappa })\) be the left adjoint of \(\colim _{\kappa }\), and let \(F\) be as in the proof of (2) \(\implies \) (3). Since \(\colim _C \circ F\) preserves filtered colimits and restricts to the inclusion on \(C^{\kappa }\), we have \(\colim _C \circ F \simeq \colim _{\kappa }\), and hence \[ \Map _{\Ind (C^{\kappa })}(X,F^Ry(c)) \simeq \Map _{\Ind (C)}(FX,y(c)) \simeq \Map _C(\colim _C FX,c) \simeq \Map _C(\colim _{\kappa }X,c). \] So \(\colim _{\kappa }\) admits the right adjoint \(F^R \circ y\), which is fully faithful since \(\colim _{\kappa }F^Ry(c) \simeq c\) as shown above. Hence \(\hat {y}\) is fully faithful by Lemma A.26. Its right adjoint \(\colim _{\kappa }\) preserves colimits, so \(\hat {y}\) is strongly continuous, and \(\Ind (C^{\kappa })\) is compactly generated.

(5) \(\implies \) (1): Let \(i\colon C \hookrightarrow D\) be as in (5). Then \(i^R i \simeq \id _C\) and \(i^R\) preserves colimits, so \(C\) is a retract of \(D\) in \(\PrL _{\st }\). Since \(D\) is dualizable (Corollary A.32) and dualizable objects are closed under retracts, \(C\) is dualizable. □

Remark 3.18. Further characterizations of dualizable categories will be given later in these notes:

  • \(C\) is equivalent to \(\ker (\Mod _A \to \Mod _B)\) for a homological epimorphism of \(\bbE _1\)-rings \(A \to B\) (Chapter 5);
  • \(C\) is generated under colimits by compactly exhaustible objects, i.e. sequential colimits along compact morphisms (Proposition 4.14);
  • \(C\) satisfies (AB6), i.e. filtered colimits distribute over products in \(C\) (Proposition 4.21);
  • \(C\) is flat, i.e. \(C \otimes -\) preserves fully faithful functors in \(\PrL _{\st }\) (Chapter 10).
