Calkin categories

← Previous chapterChapter 1 → Next chapterChapter 3 ← Previous sectionSection 2.4 → Next sectionSection 2.6

For \(A \in \Cat _{\perf }\) and a regular cardinal \(\kappa \), we write \(\Ind (A)^{\kappa }\) for the full subcategory of \(\kappa \)-compact objects of \(\Ind (A)\). It is essentially small and stable, and it contains \(A = \Ind (A)^{\omega }\) as a thick subcategory.

Definition 2.26 ([Efimov (2024), Section 2.9]). Let \(A \in \Cat _{\perf }\) and let \(\kappa \) be an uncountable regular cardinal. The \(\kappa \)-Calkin category of \(A\) is \[ \Calk _{\kappa }(A) := \big (\Ind (A)^{\kappa }/A\big )^{\idem }, \] so that there is a short exact sequence \(A \hookrightarrow \Ind (A)^{\kappa } \to \Calk _{\kappa }(A)\).

Remark 2.27. The mapping spectra in \(\Calk _{\kappa }(A)\) can be computed using Remark 2.8. Let \(x = \quotcolim _{i \in I} x_i\) and \(y = \quotcolim _{j \in J} y_j\) be objects of \(\Ind (A)^{\kappa }\), with \(I\) and \(J\) filtered. The functor \(J \to A_{/y}\) sending \(j\) to \(y_j \to y\) is cofinal, and \(\hom _{\Ind (A)}(x,y_j) \simeq \lim _i \hom _A(x_i,y_j)\). Hence \[ \hom _{\Calk _{\kappa }(A)}(x,y) \simeq \cofib \Big (\colim _{j}\lim _{i}\hom _A(x_i,y_j) \to \lim _{i}\colim _{j}\hom _A(x_i,y_j)\Big ). \] So the Calkin category measures the failure of the limit over \(i\) to commute with the filtered colimit over \(j\).

To justify the cofinality assertion, fix \(a \to y\) with \(a \in A\). The comma category \((a \to y)/J\) is the category of elements of the functor \[ j \mapsto \fib _{a \to y}\big (\Map _A(a,y_j) \to \Map _{\Ind (A)}(a,y)\big ). \] Its groupoid completion is the colimit of this functor. Since \(a\) is compact and filtered colimits of animae commute with finite limits, this colimit is the fiber of the identity of \(\Map _{\Ind (A)}(a,y)\) over \(a \to y\), hence is contractible. The cofinality criterion therefore applies.

Example 2.28. Let \(R\) be an \(\bbE _{\infty }\)-ring and \(A = \Perf _R\), so that \(\Ind (A) \simeq \Mod _R\). For \(M \in \Mod _R\) and a perfect \(R\)-module \(P\) we have \(\hom _R(M,P) \simeq \hom _R(M,R) \otimes _R P\), since \(P\) is dualizable. Writing \(M' \in \Mod _R\) as a filtered colimit of perfect modules, Remark 2.8 gives \[ \hom _{\Calk _{\kappa }(\Perf _R)}(M,M') \simeq \cofib \big (\hom _R(M,R) \otimes _R M' \to \hom _R(M,M')\big ) \] for \(M,M' \in \Mod _R^{\kappa }\). For instance, if \(R = k\) is a field and \(V\) is a \(k\)-vector space of dimension \(< \kappa \), the map \(V^{\vee } \otimes _k V \to \End _k(V)\) is injective with image the finite rank endomorphisms, so \[ \End _{\Calk _{\kappa }(\Perf _k)}(V) \simeq \End _k(V)/\{\text {finite rank endomorphisms}\}. \] This is the algebraic analogue of the Calkin algebra \(B(\Hh )/\Kk (\Hh )\) of bounded operators modulo compact operators on a Hilbert space, which explains the name.
