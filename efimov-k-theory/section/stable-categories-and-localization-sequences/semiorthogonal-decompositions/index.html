Semiorthogonal decompositions

← Previous chapterChapter 1 → Next chapterChapter 3 ← Previous sectionSection 2.3 → Next sectionSection 2.5

The simplest localization sequences are the split ones. In this case the middle category \(E\) is glued from two subcategories \(C\) and \(D\) between which there are no maps in one direction, and every object of \(E\) is uniquely an extension of an object of \(C\) by an object of \(D\). Such a splitting is called a semiorthogonal decomposition. We follow Efimov’s convention: in \(E = \lra {C_1,\dots ,C_n}\) there are no nonzero maps from \(C_i\) to \(C_j\) for \(i > j\). We first treat the presentable setting, which is the one relevant for dualizable categories, and then explain the analogous notion for small stable categories and its relation to short exact sequences.

Definition 2.21 ([Efimov (2024), Definition 2.74]). A semiorthogonal decomposition of a presentable stable category \(E\) is a collection of full presentable stable subcategories \(C_1,\dots ,C_n \subseteq E\) such that

(1)

the inclusion functors \(C_i \hookrightarrow E\) are strongly continuous;

(2)

for \(i > j\), \(x \in C_i\) and \(y \in C_j\) we have \(\hom _E(x,y) \simeq 0\);

(3)

the smallest full stable subcategory of \(E\) containing \(C_1,\dots ,C_n\) is \(E\) itself.

In this case we write \(E = \lra {C_1,\dots ,C_n}\).

Proposition 2.22. Let \(E = \lra {C,D}\) be a semiorthogonal decomposition, with inclusions \(i_C\colon C \hookrightarrow E\) and \(i_D\colon D \hookrightarrow E\). Then:

(1)

Every \(x \in E\) fits into a cofiber sequence \(d \to x \to c\) with \(d \in D\) and \(c \in C\), which is unique up to unique equivalence and functorial in \(x\).

(2)

The functor \(i_C\) admits a left adjoint \(i_C^L\) and \(i_D\) admits a right adjoint \(i_D^R\), given by \(x \mapsto c\) and \(x \mapsto d\) respectively; there is a natural cofiber sequence \(i_Di_D^R \to \id _E \to i_Ci_C^L\), and \(i_C^Li_D \simeq 0\).

(3)

The sequence \(D \xrightarrow {i_D} E \xrightarrow {i_C^L} C\) is a strongly continuous localization sequence, split by \(i_C\).

Proof.

Note first that \(\hom _E(d,c) \simeq 0\) for \(d \in D\) and \(c \in C\). Suppose \(d \to x \to c\) is a cofiber sequence with \(d \in D\) and \(c \in C\). For \(d' \in D\) we then have \(\hom _E(d',d) \xrightarrow {\simeq } \hom _E(d',x)\), since \(\hom _E(d',c) \simeq 0\); so \(d \to x\) exhibits \(d\) as the value at \(x\) of a right adjoint to \(i_D\). Dually, for \(c' \in C\) we have \(\hom _E(c,c') \xrightarrow {\simeq } \hom _E(x,c')\), so \(x \to c\) exhibits \(c\) as the value at \(x\) of a left adjoint of \(i_C\). In particular, such a sequence is unique up to unique equivalence and functorial in \(x\), wherever it exists.

Let \(E' \subseteq E\) be the full subcategory of those \(x\) that admit such a cofiber sequence. It contains \(C\) (take \(0 \to c \to c\)) and \(D\) (take \(d \to d \to 0\)) and is closed under shifts. It is closed under cofibers: a map \(x \to x'\) in \(E'\) induces, by the universal properties above, a map of cofiber sequences, and passing to cofibers gives a cofiber sequence \(\cofib (d \to d') \to \cofib (x \to x') \to \cofib (c \to c')\) with first term in \(D\) and last term in \(C\), as these are stable subcategories. Hence \(E'\) is a stable subcategory containing \(C\) and \(D\), so \(E' = E\) by assumption (3). This proves (1), and by [Lurie (2009), Proposition 5.2.4.2] also the existence of the adjoints in (2), together with the cofiber sequence \(i_Di_D^R \to \id \to i_Ci_C^L\). Applying it to \(x = i_D(d)\) gives \(i_C^Li_D(d) \simeq 0\).

For (3), \(i_C^L\) is a localization since its right adjoint \(i_C\) is fully faithful, and \(i_C^L(x) \simeq 0\) if and only if \(i_Di_D^R x \to x\) is an equivalence, if and only if \(x\) lies in the essential image of \(D\). The right adjoints \(i_D^R\) and \(i_C\) preserve colimits by assumption (1) and since \(C \subseteq E\) is closed under colimits, so the sequence is strongly continuous. Finally, \(i_C^L i_C \simeq \id _C\) as \(i_C\) is fully faithful, so \(i_C\) splits \(i_C^L\). □

Conversely, a semiorthogonal decomposition can be recognized from the adjoints of the inclusion functors. In particular, the projections onto the two pieces are themselves strongly continuous.

Proposition 2.23 ([Efimov (2024), Remark 2.75]). Let \(i_1\colon C \to E\) and \(i_2\colon D \to E\) be strongly continuous functors between presentable stable categories. Then \(i_1\) and \(i_2\) are fully faithful and exhibit \(E = \lra {i_1(C), i_2(D)}\) as a semiorthogonal decomposition if and only if the following conditions hold:

(1)

\(i_1\) admits a left adjoint \(i_1^L\) and \(i_2\) admits a right adjoint \(i_2^R\);

(2)

the counit \(i_1^Li_1 \to \id _C\) and the unit \(\id _D \to i_2^Ri_2\) are equivalences;

(3)

\(i_1^Li_2 \simeq 0\), or equivalently \(i_2^Ri_1 \simeq 0\);

(4)

the sequence \(i_2i_2^R \to \id _E \to i_1i_1^L\), formed by the counit and the unit, is a cofiber sequence.

In this case, the functors \(i_1^L\) and \(i_2^R\) are strongly continuous.

The composite in (4) admits a unique nullhomotopy, as \(\Map (i_2i_2^R,i_1i_1^L) \simeq \Map (i_1^Li_2i_2^R,i_1^L) \simeq *\) by (3); so the condition in (4) makes sense. The equivalence of the two formulations of (3) follows from \(\hom _D(d,i_2^Ri_1c) \simeq \hom _E(i_2d,i_1c) \simeq \hom _C(i_1^Li_2d,c)\).

Proof.

Suppose first that \(i_1\) and \(i_2\) are fully faithful and \(E = \lra {i_1(C),i_2(D)}\). Then (1), (3) and (4) are Proposition 2.22(2), applied to the essential images, and (2) holds as \(i_1\) and \(i_2\) are fully faithful.

Conversely, assume (1)–(4). By (2), the functors \(i_1\) and \(i_2\) are fully faithful. For \(c \in C\) and \(d \in D\) we have \(\hom _E(i_2d,i_1c) \simeq \hom _C(i_1^Li_2d,c) \simeq 0\) by (3), so the semiorthogonality condition holds. For \(e \in E\), the cofiber sequence \(i_2i_2^Re \to e \to i_1i_1^Le\) of (4) shows that \(e\) lies in the stable subcategory generated by the essential images of \(i_1\) and \(i_2\). Finally, the inclusions are strongly continuous by assumption, and their essential images are closed under colimits, as \(i_1\) and \(i_2\) preserve colimits.

It remains to show that \(i_1^L\) and \(i_2^R\) are strongly continuous. The right adjoint of \(i_1^L\) is \(i_1\), which preserves colimits. The right adjoint of \(i_1i_1^L\) is \(i_1i_1^R\), which preserves colimits since \(i_1\) is strongly continuous. Passing to right adjoints is an equivalence between the stable category of colimit-preserving endofunctors of \(E\) and the opposite of the stable category of accessible limit-preserving endofunctors, so it carries the fiber sequence \(i_2i_2^R \to \id _E \to i_1i_1^L\) to a cofiber sequence \(i_1i_1^R \to \id _E \to (i_2i_2^R)^R\). Hence \((i_2i_2^R)^R\) preserves colimits. Since \(i_2\) is fully faithful, we have \[ \hom _D(i_2^Re,d) \simeq \hom _E(i_2i_2^Re,i_2d) \simeq \hom _E(e,(i_2i_2^R)^R i_2 d), \] so \((i_2i_2^R)^R \circ i_2\) is right adjoint to \(i_2^R\) and preserves colimits. □

Remark 2.24. Definition 2.21 makes sense for any stable category \(E\) and full stable subcategories \(C_1,\dots ,C_n \subseteq E\), omitting condition (1). No presentability or size assumption is needed. Parts (1) and (2) of Proposition 2.22 hold in this setting with the same proof, and the sequence \(D \xrightarrow {i_D} E \xrightarrow {i_C^L} C\) is a Verdier sequence: \(i_C^L\) is a localization with kernel \(D\), and it inverts precisely the maps whose cofiber lies in \(D\). If \(E\) is idempotent complete, then so are \(C\) and \(D\), being thick subcategories of \(E\) (as kernels of the exact functors \(i_D^R\) and \(i_C^L\)), and the sequence is short exact. (In the talk, the definition was given simultaneously for presentable stable categories, for dualizable categories and for small stable categories.)

Conversely, short exact sequences admitting adjoints give rise to semiorthogonal decompositions.

Proposition 2.25. Let \(A \xrightarrow {i} B \xrightarrow {p} C\) be a short exact sequence in \(\Cat _{\perf }\). Then \(i\) admits a right adjoint \(i^R\) if and only if \(p\) admits a right adjoint \(p^R\). In this case:

(1)

The functor \(p^R\) is fully faithful, with essential image the right orthogonal \(A^{\perp } = \{x \in B \mid \hom _B(ia,x) \simeq 0 \text { for all } a \in A\}\);

(2)

There is a semiorthogonal decomposition \(B = \lra {p^R(C),\, i(A)}\);

(3)

The sequence \(C \xrightarrow {p^R} B \xrightarrow {i^R} A\) is again short exact.

Proof.

Assume first that \(i^R\) exists, and for \(b \in B\) let \(L(b) := \cofib (ii^Rb \to b)\). As in the proof of Proposition 2.12(1), \(L(b) \in A^{\perp }\) and \(L\colon B \to A^{\perp }\) is left adjoint to the inclusion. Moreover, \(L(b) \simeq 0\) if and only if \(b \simeq ii^Rb\) lies in \(A\), so \(L\) inverts precisely the maps whose cofiber lies in \(A\). Since \(L\) is a reflective localization, it exhibits \(A^{\perp }\) as the localization of \(B\) at these maps [Lurie (2009), Proposition 5.2.7.12], so \(B/A \simeq A^{\perp }\). As \(A^{\perp }\) is closed under retracts in the idempotent complete category \(B\), it is idempotent complete, so \(C \simeq (B/A)^{\idem } \simeq A^{\perp }\), with \(p\) corresponding to \(L\). Hence \(p\) admits the fully faithful right adjoint \(A^{\perp } \hookrightarrow B\).

Conversely, assume that \(p^R\) exists. For \(a \in A\) and \(c \in C\) we have \(\hom _B(ia,p^Rc) \simeq \hom _C(pia,c) \simeq 0\), so \(p^R\) lands in \(A^{\perp }\). Every \(x \in A^{\perp }\) is local with respect to the maps \(s\colon b' \to b\) with \(\cofib (s) \in A\), i.e. \(s^*\colon \hom _B(b,x) \to \hom _B(b',x)\) is an equivalence. Hence \(\hom _{B/A}(qb,qx) \simeq \hom _B(b,x)\) for all \(b \in B\) and \(x \in A^{\perp }\) (for instance by Remark 2.8, applied to \(B\catop \)). Applying this to \(x = p^Rc\), we find that the composite \[ \hom _B(b,p^Rc) \to \hom _C(pb,pp^Rc) \to \hom _C(pb,c), \] whose second map is induced by the counit, is the adjunction equivalence. Its first map is an equivalence as well: since the functor \(B/A \to C\) induced by \(p\) is fully faithful, it is identified with the equivalence \(\hom _B(b,p^Rc) \simeq \hom _{B/A}(qb,qp^Rc)\). Hence so is the second map, i.e. the counit \(pp^Rc \to c\) induces an equivalence on \(\hom _C(pb,-)\) for all \(b \in B\). Since every object of \(C\) is a retract of an object of the form \(pb\), the counit is an equivalence, i.e. \(p^R\) is fully faithful. For \(b \in B\), let \(f \to b \to p^Rpb\) be the fiber sequence of the unit. Then \(p(f) \simeq 0\), so \(f\) lies in \(A\), and for \(a \in A\) we get \(\hom _B(ia,f) \simeq \hom _B(ia,b)\) since \(\hom _B(ia,p^Rpb) \simeq 0\). Hence \(b \mapsto f\) is right adjoint to \(i\).

It remains to prove (1)–(3). For (1), it remains to show that every \(x \in A^{\perp }\) lies in the image of \(p^R\). The fiber \(f\) of the unit \(x \to p^Rpx\) lies in \(A\), so \(\hom _B(f,x) \simeq 0\) and \(\hom _B(f,p^Rpx) \simeq 0\), whence \(\hom _B(f,f) \simeq 0\) and \(f \simeq 0\). For (2), we have \(\hom _B(ia,p^Rc) \simeq 0\), and every \(b \in B\) fits into the cofiber sequence \(ii^Rb \to b \to p^Rpb\). For (3), the kernel of \(i^R\) is \(A^{\perp } = p^R(C)\) by (1), and \(i^R\) is a coreflective localization since its left adjoint \(i\) is fully faithful; applying the first paragraph to opposite categories, \(i^R\) exhibits \(A\) as the Verdier quotient \(B/p^R(C)\). □
