Filtered colimits of dualizable categories

← Previous chapterChapter 6 → Next chapterAppendix A ← Previous sectionSection 7.2 → Next sectionSection 7.4

Filtered colimits in \(\Cat _{\perf }\) are exact: they preserve fully faithful functors and short exact sequences. The analogous statement fails in \(\PrL _{\st }\) (Remark 7.13). We now show that it does hold in \(\Pr ^{\mathrm {LL}}_{\st }\) and in \(\Pr ^{\dual }\), whose colimits are computed in \(\PrL _{\st }\) by Section 7.1. As a consequence, the functor \(C \mapsto C^{\omega }\) on \(\Pr ^{\dual }\) preserves filtered colimits. By Theorem A.8, every filtered colimit can be computed as a colimit over a directed poset, so we may restrict attention to directed posets.

Lemma 7.10 ([Efimov (2024), Proposition 2.65]). Let \(I\) be a directed poset, let \(I \to \PrL _{\st }\), \(i \mapsto C_i\), be a diagram with transition functors \(F_{ij}\colon C_i \to C_j\), let \(C := \colim _i C_i\) in \(\PrL _{\st }\), and let \(F_i\colon C_i \to C\) be the structure functors.

(1)

The counits induce an equivalence \(\colim _i F_iF_i^R \simeq \id _C\).

(2)

If the functors \(F_{ij}\) are strongly continuous, then for all \(i,j \in I\) there is a natural equivalence \[ \colim _{k \geq i,j} F_{jk}^RF_{ik} \simeq F_j^RF_i. \]

Proof.

We identify \(C\) with \(\lim _i C_i\), taken along the functors \(F_{ij}^R\), so that \(F_i^R\) becomes the projection. Mapping spectra in this limit are the limits of the mapping spectra.

(1) For \(i \leq j\), the counit of \(F_{ij} \dashv F_{ij}^R\) induces a map \(F_iF_i^R \simeq F_jF_{ij}F_{ij}^RF_j^R \to F_jF_j^R\), so the functors \(F_iF_i^R\) form an \(I\)-indexed diagram with a cocone to \(\id _C\), given by the counits. For \(x,y \in C\) we get natural equivalences \[ \hom _C\big (\colim _i F_iF_i^Rx,y\big ) \simeq \lim _i \hom _C(F_iF_i^Rx,y) \simeq \lim _i \hom _{C_i}(F_i^Rx,F_i^Ry) \simeq \hom _C(x,y), \] whose composite is induced by the cocone. By the Yoneda lemma, the cocone exhibits \(\id _C\) as the colimit.

(2) By Proposition 7.1(1), the functors \(F_i\) are strongly continuous, so the projections \(F_j^R\) preserve colimits, and the transition functors \(F_{jj'}^R\) of the limit preserve colimits by assumption. Fix \(i\), and for \(x \in C_i\) consider the family of objects \[ F_i'(x)_j := \colim _{k \geq i,j} F_{jk}^RF_{ik}(x) \in C_j, \qquad j \in I. \] For \(k \leq \ell \), the transition map in this colimit is \[ F_{jk}^RF_{ik} \longrightarrow F_{jk}^RF_{k\ell }^RF_{k\ell }F_{ik} \simeq F_{j\ell }^RF_{i\ell }, \] induced by the unit of \(F_{k\ell } \dashv F_{k\ell }^R\). The units for composites give these diagrams coherently. For \(j \leq j'\), we have \(F_{jj'}^R F_i'(x)_{j'} \simeq \colim _{k \geq i,j'} F_{jk}^RF_{ik}(x) \simeq F_i'(x)_j\), using that \(F_{jj'}^R\) preserves colimits and that \(\{k \geq i,j'\} \subseteq \{k \geq i,j\}\) is cofinal. These identifications are induced by restriction to common cofinal tails and the composition isomorphisms for right adjoints. Their compatibility for chains of indices follows from that of the units above and the universal property of colimits. Thus they define a functor \(F_i'\colon C_i \to \lim _j C_j \simeq C\), and the claim is equivalent to \(F_i' \simeq F_i\). We show that \(F_i'\) is left adjoint to \(F_i^R\). For \(x \in C_i\) and \(y = (y_j)_j \in C\), we have \begin {align*} \hom _C(F_i'x,y) &\simeq \lim _j \hom _{C_j}\Big (\colim _{k \geq i,j} F_{jk}^RF_{ik}x,\, y_j\Big ) \\ &\simeq \lim _{(j,k) \in P\catop } \hom _{C_j}(F_{jk}^RF_{ik}x,y_j) \\ &\simeq \lim _{j \geq i} \hom _{C_j}(F_{ij}x,y_j) \simeq \lim _{j \geq i} \hom _{C_i}(x,F_{ij}^Ry_j) \\ &\simeq \hom _{C_i}(x,y_i) = \hom _{C_i}(x,F_i^Ry). \end {align*}

Here \(P = \{(j,k) \mid i \leq j \leq k\}\), with the product order. In the second step we first restrict the limit over \(j\) to the cofinal subset \(I_{\geq i}\). The functor \((j,k) \mapsto \hom _{C_j}(F_{jk}^RF_{ik}x,y_j)\) is contravariant in \((j,k)\). In the third step we restrict along the diagonal \(I_{\geq i} \to P\), \(j \mapsto (j,j)\), which is cofinal: for \((j,k) \in P\), the poset of \(m \geq i\) with \((j,k) \leq (m,m)\) is \(I_{\geq k}\), which is directed and hence weakly contractible. Thus the opposite of the diagonal is initial, as required for this limit over \(P\catop \). On the diagonal, \(F_{jj}^R = \id \). The fourth step is the adjunction \(F_{ij} \dashv F_{ij}^R\), and the remaining diagram is constant with value \(\hom _{C_i}(x,y_i)\), as \(F_{ij}^Ry_j \simeq y_i\). The unit of the resulting adjunction \(F_i' \dashv F_i^R\) is the map \(x \to F_i'(x)_i\) from the summand \(k=i\). To identify the resulting isomorphism with the canonical map, use the cocones \[ F_{jk}^RF_{ik} \longrightarrow F_{jk}^RF_k^RF_kF_{ik} \simeq F_j^RF_i, \] induced by the unit of \(F_k \dashv F_k^R\). They define \(F_i' \to F_i\), and at \(j=k=i\) carry the unit just described to the unit of \(F_i \dashv F_i^R\). By the adjunction, this map is the isomorphism between the two left adjoints. Evaluating at the \(j\)-th component proves the claim with its canonical cocone. □

Proposition 7.11 ([Efimov (2024), Proposition 2.67]).

(1)

In \(\Pr ^{\mathrm {LL}}_{\st }\) and in \(\Pr ^{\dual }\), the class of fully faithful functors is closed under filtered colimits.

(2)

A filtered colimit of strongly continuous localization sequences, formed in \(\PrL _{\st }\), is again a strongly continuous localization sequence. In particular, filtered colimits of short exact sequences of dualizable categories are short exact.

Proof.

(1) By Proposition 7.2, it suffices to treat \(\Pr ^{\mathrm {LL}}_{\st }\). Let \(I\) be a directed poset, let \((C_i)\) and \((D_i)\) be \(I\)-indexed diagrams in \(\Pr ^{\mathrm {LL}}_{\st }\) with transition functors \(F_{ij}\) and \(G_{ij}\), and let \(\Phi _i\colon C_i \to D_i\) be a natural transformation whose components are fully faithful and strongly continuous. Let \(C = \colim _i C_i\) and \(D = \colim _i D_i\), with structure functors \(F_i\) and \(G_i\), and let \(\Phi \colon C \to D\) be the induced functor, which is strongly continuous by Proposition 7.1. We show that the unit \(\id _C \to \Phi ^R\Phi \) is an equivalence. Both functors preserve colimits, the essential images of the \(F_i\) generate \(C\) under colimits, and the functors \(F_j^R\) are jointly conservative (see the proof of Proposition 7.2). So it suffices to show that \(F_j^RF_i \to F_j^R\Phi ^R\Phi F_i\) is an equivalence for all \(i,j \in I\). Using \(\Phi F_i \simeq G_i\Phi _i\), Lemma 7.10(2), the strong continuity of \(\Phi _j\) and the fully faithfulness of \(\Phi _k\), we compute \begin {align*} F_j^R\Phi ^R\Phi F_i &\simeq \Phi _j^RG_j^RG_i\Phi _i \simeq \Phi _j^R\Big (\colim _{k \geq i,j} G_{jk}^RG_{ik}\Big )\Phi _i \simeq \colim _{k \geq i,j} \Phi _j^RG_{jk}^RG_{ik}\Phi _i \\ &\simeq \colim _{k \geq i,j} F_{jk}^R\Phi _k^R\Phi _kF_{ik} \simeq \colim _{k \geq i,j} F_{jk}^RF_{ik} \simeq F_j^RF_i. \end {align*}

More precisely, under the canonical cocones in Lemma 7.10(2), the map \(F_j^RF_i \to F_j^R\Phi ^R\Phi F_i\) is the colimit of the maps \[ F_{jk}^RF_{ik} \longrightarrow F_{jk}^R\Phi _k^R\Phi _kF_{ik} \] induced by the units of \(\Phi _k \dashv \Phi _k^R\). This follows by pasting the adjunction units for the squares \(\Phi _kF_{ik} \simeq G_{ik}\Phi _i\) and for the colimit cocones. Each of these maps is an isomorphism because \(\Phi _k\) is fully faithful, so their colimit is an isomorphism as required.

(2) Let \(C_i \to D_i \to E_i\) be a filtered diagram of strongly continuous localization sequences, and let \(C \to D \to E\) be its colimit in \(\PrL _{\st }\). By Proposition 7.1, it is a sequence of strongly continuous functors. Since colimits commute with colimits, \(D \to E\) is the cofiber of \(C \to D\) in \(\PrL _{\st }\). By (1), \(C \to D\) is fully faithful, and it preserves colimits. Hence \(C \to D \to E\) is a localization sequence by Proposition 2.12(1). If the \(D_i\) are dualizable, then so is \(D\) by Proposition 7.2. □

Corollary 7.12 ([Efimov (2024), Corollary 2.68]). Let \(C\) be a presentable stable category, and let \((C_i)_{i \in I}\) be a directed family of presentable stable full subcategories \(C_i \subseteq C\) whose inclusion functors are strongly continuous. If \(C\) is generated under colimits by the \(C_i\), then the functor \(\colim _i C_i \to C\) is an equivalence, where the colimit is formed in \(\PrL _{\st }\).

Proof.

For \(i \leq j\), the right adjoint of \(C_i \hookrightarrow C_j\) is the restriction of the right adjoint of \(C_i \hookrightarrow C\), so the transition functors are strongly continuous. The colimit of the constant diagram with value \(C\) over the weakly contractible poset \(I\) is \(C\). Applying Proposition 7.11(1) to the inclusions \(C_i \hookrightarrow C\) shows that \(\colim _i C_i \to C\) is fully faithful. Its essential image is closed under colimits and contains all \(C_i\), so it is all of \(C\). □

Remark 7.13. Proposition 7.11 and Corollary 7.12 fail in \(\PrL _{\st }\), i.e. without the strong continuity assumptions. Efimov [Efimov (2024), Remark 2.69] considers \(C = \D (\Z )\) and \(C_n = \D (\Z [p_k^{-1} \mid k \geq n]) \subseteq C\), where \(p_k\) is the \(k\)-th prime number. The subcategories \(C_1 \subseteq C_2 \subseteq \cdots \) generate \(C\) under colimits, but \(\colim _n C_n \to C\) is not an equivalence, as one shows using Lemma 7.10(1).

To compute compact objects in filtered colimits, we use the following observation. Let \(C \to \Ind (A) \to \Ind (B)\) be a short exact sequence of dualizable categories, where \(A, B \in \Cat _{\perf }\) and the second functor is \(\Ind (p)\) for a functor \(p\colon A \to B\). Then \begin {equation} \tag{7.1}\label {eq:Compacts_Of_Kernel} C^{\omega } \simeq \ker (p\colon A \to B). \end {equation} Indeed, a fully faithful strongly continuous functor preserves compact objects, and it detects them since it preserves filtered colimits and is fully faithful. So \(C^{\omega }\) consists of the compact objects of \(\Ind (A)\), i.e. the objects of \(A\), which lie in \(C = \ker (\Ind (p))\).

We recall the categorical facts used below. Limits and filtered colimits of stable categories and exact functors are computed in \(\Cat \) [Lurie (2017), Theorem 1.1.4.4 and Proposition 1.1.4.6]. The same holds for \(\Cat _{\perf }\): idempotent completeness is closed under limits; in a filtered colimit, an idempotent in the homotopy category is represented by an endomorphism at some stage, and its idempotence holds at a later stage, where it splits. Here we use the characterization of idempotent completeness of a stable category by idempotent completeness of its homotopy category.

Filtered colimits in \(\Cat \) commute with finite limits, and taking the core preserves filtered colimits. One can check these two facts in the quasicategory model: filtered colimits are computed on underlying simplicial sets; an edge is invertible precisely when it has a two-sided inverse in the homotopy category, a condition witnessed by finite data. Thus taking the maximal Kan complex commutes with filtered colimits. Pullbacks of categories can be computed by triples \((x,y,u)\) with \(u\) an isomorphism between the two images; this construction, as well as finite products, commutes with filtered colimits. Consequently filtered colimits in \(\Cat _{\perf }\) commute with finite limits as well.

Proposition 7.14 ([Efimov (2024), Proposition 2.70]). The functor \((-)^{\omega }\colon \Pr ^{\dual } \to \Cat _{\perf }\) preserves filtered colimits. In particular, \(\Sp \) is a compact object of \(\Pr ^{\dual }\).

Proof.

Let \((C_i)_{i \in I}\) be a filtered diagram in \(\Pr ^{\dual }\), and put \(C := \colim _i C_i\), \(A := \colim _i C_i^{\omega _1}\) and \(B := \colim _i \Calk ^{\cont }_{\omega _1}(C_i)\), the latter two colimits taken in \(\Cat _{\perf }\). The functor \(\Ind \colon \Cat _{\perf } \to \PrL _{\st }\) preserves colimits: for \(T \in \PrL _{\st }\), we have \(\Fun ^{\colim }(\Ind (A'),T) \simeq \Fun ^{\ex }(A',T)\) by Theorem A.15(3), and \(\Fun ^{\ex }(-,T)\) sends colimits in \(\Cat _{\perf }\) to limits, since \(T\) is idempotent complete. By Proposition 7.9, Proposition 7.11(2), applied to the Calkin sequences of the \(C_i\) for \(\kappa = \omega _1\), we therefore obtain a short exact sequence \[ C \to \Ind (A) \to \Ind (B). \] By (7.1), applied to this sequence and to the Calkin sequences of the \(C_i\), we get \[ C^{\omega } \simeq \ker (A \to B) \simeq \colim _i \ker \big (C_i^{\omega _1} \to \Calk ^{\cont }_{\omega _1}(C_i)\big ) \simeq \colim _i C_i^{\omega }, \] where the middle equivalence uses the commutation of filtered colimits with finite limits established above.

Finally, a colimit-preserving functor \(\Sp \to C\) is determined by the image \(x\) of \(\S \), and it is strongly continuous if and only if its right adjoint \(\hom _C(x,-)\) preserves colimits, i.e. if and only if \(x\) is compact. So \(\Map _{\Pr ^{\dual }}(\Sp ,C) \simeq (C^{\omega })^{\simeq }\). Since \((-)^{\simeq }\colon \Cat _{\perf } \to \An \) preserves filtered colimits, \(\Sp \) is compact in \(\Pr ^{\dual }\). □

To see why the core functor is conservative on stable categories and exact functors, observe that \(\Map _C(x,y)\) is a retract of the anima of automorphisms of \(x \oplus y\): send \(f\) to the shear \(\left (\begin {smallmatrix}1&0\\f&1\end {smallmatrix}\right )\), and send an automorphism \(u\) to \(p_yui_x\). These maps are natural under exact functors. A functor inducing an isomorphism on cores therefore induces isomorphisms on these automorphism animae and hence on their retracts \(\Map _C(x,y)\); it is also essentially surjective, so it is an equivalence.

Conversely, compactness of \(\Sp \) implies that \((-)^{\omega }\) preserves filtered colimits, since \((-)^{\simeq }\colon \Cat _{\perf } \to \An \) is conservative [Efimov (2024), Proposition 2.70].
