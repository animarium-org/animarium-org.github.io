Finite limits of dualizable categories

← Previous chapterChapter 6 → Next chapterAppendix A ← Previous sectionSection 7.3 → Next sectionSection A.1

Limits in \(\Pr ^{\dual }\) are much more complicated than colimits. In general, the kernel in \(\PrL _{\st }\) of a strongly continuous functor between dualizable categories is not dualizable, or its inclusion is not strongly continuous (Example 7.20). Instead, the kernel in \(\Pr ^{\dual }\) is the largest dualizable subcategory of the kernel whose inclusion is strongly continuous. The existence of such a largest subcategory relies on the following refinement of the description of \(\omega _1\)-compact objects in terms of compact morphisms.

Proposition 7.15 ([Efimov (2024), Proposition 2.81]). Let \(C\) be a dualizable category and let \(x \in C^{\omega _1}\). Then there exists a functor \(\Phi \colon (\Q ,\leq ) \to C\) such that

(1)

\(\colim _{a \in \Q } \Phi (a) \simeq x\);

(2)

for every \(a \in \Q \), the canonical map \(\colim _{b < a} \Phi (b) \to \Phi (a)\) is an equivalence;

(3)

for all \(a < b\) in \(\Q \), the morphism \(\Phi (a) \to \Phi (b)\) is compact.

Proof.

By Proposition 4.8(3), there is a functor \(F\colon \Q \to C\) with \(\colim _{\Q } F \simeq x\) such that \(F(a) \to F(b)\) is compact for all \(a < b\). Define \(\Phi (a) := \colim _{b < a} F(b)\); formally, \(\Phi \) is the left Kan extension of \(F \circ \mathrm {pr}_1\) along \(\mathrm {pr}_2\colon \{(b,a) \in \Q ^2 \mid b < a\} \to \Q \). For \(a < a'\), choose \(c\) with \(a < c < a'\). Then \(\Phi (a) \to \Phi (a')\) factors as \[ \Phi (a) \to F(a) \to F(c) \to \Phi (a'), \] so it is compact, since \(F(a) \to F(c)\) is compact and compact morphisms form an ideal (Remark 3.4). Since \(\Q \) is dense, we have \(\colim _{b < a}\Phi (b) \simeq \colim _{b < a}\colim _{c < b} F(c) \simeq \colim _{c < a} F(c) = \Phi (a)\), and similarly \(\colim _{\Q }\Phi \simeq \colim _{\Q } F \simeq x\). □

In the talk, the functor \(F\) was constructed directly: one extends a compact exhaustion \(x_1 \to x_2 \to \cdots \) of \(x\) by zero to a functor on \(\Z \) and then refines it to the dyadic rationals by repeatedly factoring the transition maps, as in the proof of Lemma 4.7.

Lemma 7.16 ([Efimov (2024), Lemma 2.82]). Let \(C\) be a dualizable category, and let \(D \subseteq C\) be a full stable subcategory closed under colimits. Then there is a largest full subcategory \(E \subseteq D\) such that \(E\) is dualizable and the inclusion \(E \hookrightarrow C\) is strongly continuous. Moreover, \(E\) is generated under colimits by the objects \(\colim _{\Q } F\), where \(F\colon \Q \to D\) is a functor such that \(F(a) \to F(b)\) is compact in \(C\) for all \(a < b\).

Proof.

Let \(E' \subseteq D\) be the full stable subcategory generated under colimits by the objects \(\colim _{\Q } F\) as in the statement. Each such object is the colimit of the sequence \(F(0) \to F(1) \to \cdots \) of compact morphisms, hence is \(\omega _1\)-compact in \(C\) by Theorem 3.20(2) and Remark 3.21. As \(C^{\omega _1}\) is essentially small, \(E'\) is generated under colimits by a set of objects, and hence is presentable.

Let \(F\colon \Q \to D\) be as in the statement. Replacing \(F\) by \(a \mapsto \colim _{b < a} F(b)\) as in the proof of Proposition 7.15, which does not change \(\colim _{\Q } F\) or leave \(D\), we may assume that \(\colim _{b < a} F(b) \simeq F(a)\) for all \(a\). Then each \(F(a)\) lies in \(E'\), being the colimit of the restriction of \(F\) to \(\Q _{<a}\), which is order-isomorphic to \(\Q \). As in the proof of Proposition 3.22, we have \[ \hat {y}_C\big (\colim _{\Q } F\big ) \simeq \quotcolim \big (F(0) \to F(1) \to F(2) \to \cdots \big ), \] since the maps \(F(n) \to F(n+1)\) are compact. This lies in the essential image of the fully faithful functor \(\Ind (E') \to \Ind (C)\). This essential image is closed under colimits, since \(\Ind (E') \to \Ind (C)\) is exact and preserves filtered colimits. Since \(\hat {y}_C\) preserves colimits, it follows that \(\hat {y}_C\) maps \(E'\) into \(\Ind (E')\). For \(e \in E'\) and \(Z \in \Ind (E')\), we then have \[ \Map _{\Ind (E')}(\hat {y}_Ce,Z) \simeq \Map _{\Ind (C)}(\hat {y}_Ce,Z) \simeq \Map _C(e,\colim _C Z) \simeq \Map _{E'}(e,\colim _{E'}Z), \] as the inclusion \(E' \hookrightarrow C\) preserves colimits. So \(\colim _{E'}\colon \Ind (E') \to E'\) admits the left adjoint \(\hat {y}_C|_{E'}\), i.e. \(E'\) is compactly assembled, hence dualizable by Theorem 3.16. Moreover, the inclusion \(E' \hookrightarrow C\) commutes with \(\hat {y}\) by construction, so it is strongly continuous (Definition 3.9 and Remark 3.10).

Now let \(E'' \subseteq D\) be any full subcategory such that \(E''\) is dualizable and the inclusion \(E'' \hookrightarrow C\) is strongly continuous. Let \(x \in E''\) be \(\omega _1\)-compact. By Proposition 7.15, there is a functor \(F\colon \Q \to E''\) with \(\colim _{\Q } F \simeq x\) such that \(F(a) \to F(b)\) is compact in \(E''\) for \(a < b\). These maps are compact in \(C\) by Proposition 3.22, and \(\colim _{\Q } F \simeq x\) in \(C\) since the inclusion preserves colimits. Hence \(x \in E'\). As \(E''\) is generated under colimits by its \(\omega _1\)-compact objects (Theorem 3.20(1)), we get \(E'' \subseteq E'\). So \(E := E'\) is the largest such subcategory. □

In the talk, the strong continuity of the inclusion \(E \hookrightarrow C\) was asked about: it is precisely the compatibility \(\hat {y}_C|_{E} \simeq \hat {y}_E\) established in the proof.

Remark 7.17. Efimov also shows that the inclusion \(\Pr ^{\dual } \hookrightarrow \Pr ^{\mathrm {LL}}_{\st }\) admits a right adjoint [Efimov (2024), Remark 2.57]: for a presentable stable category \(C\), the strictly full dualizable subcategories of \(C\) with strongly continuous inclusion form a small complete lattice, whose join is given by images of products in the sense of Definition 4.27 (using Corollary 4.28 and Remark 4.26). Its largest element is the value of the right adjoint at \(C\).

Definition 7.18. Let \(F\colon C \to D\) be a strongly continuous functor between dualizable categories, and let \(\ker (F) \subseteq C\) be its kernel in \(\PrL _{\st }\). The dualizable kernel \(\ker ^{\dual }(F) \subseteq \ker (F)\) is the largest full subcategory of \(\ker (F)\) which is dualizable and whose inclusion into \(C\) is strongly continuous; it exists by Lemma 7.16.

Proposition 7.19 ([Efimov (2024), Proposition 2.83]). Let \(F\colon C \to D\) be a strongly continuous functor between dualizable categories. Then \(\ker ^{\dual }(F) \to C \xrightarrow {F} D\) is a fiber sequence in \(\Pr ^{\dual }\).

Proof.

Write \(j\colon \ker ^{\dual }(F) \hookrightarrow C\) for the inclusion. Let \(T\) be dualizable and let \(G\colon T \to C\) be strongly continuous with \(FG \simeq 0\). Then \(\Im (G) \subseteq \ker (F)\), and by Corollary 4.28, \(\Im (G)\) is dualizable and its inclusion into \(C\) is strongly continuous. By maximality, \(\Im (G) \subseteq \ker ^{\dual }(F)\), so \(G \simeq jG'\) for a colimit-preserving functor \(G'\colon T \to \ker ^{\dual }(F)\). Its right adjoint \(G^Rj\) preserves colimits, so \(G'\) is strongly continuous. Since \(j\) is fully faithful, \(j \circ -\) identifies \(\Map _{\Pr ^{\dual }}(T,\ker ^{\dual }(F))\) with the union of those path components of \(\Map _{\Pr ^{\dual }}(T,C)\) which consist of functors \(G\) with \(FG \simeq 0\). Since a nullhomotopy of \(FG\) is unique if it exists (Remark 2.3), the latter is the fiber of \(F \circ -\colon \Map _{\Pr ^{\dual }}(T,C) \to \Map _{\Pr ^{\dual }}(T,D)\) over the zero functor. □

Example 7.20 ([Efimov (2024), Example 2.84]). Let \(R\) be a discrete valuation ring with maximal ideal \(\mathfrak {m}\) and fraction field \(K\), and consider the base change functor \(F\colon \D (R) \to \D (R/\mathfrak {m})\), which is strongly continuous. Its kernel in \(\PrL _{\st }\) consists of the \(R\)-modules on which a uniformizer acts invertibly, i.e. \(\ker (F) \simeq \D (K)\). However, the inclusion \(\D (K) \hookrightarrow \D (R)\) is not strongly continuous, since its right adjoint \(\hom _R(K,-)\) does not preserve colimits, \(K\) not being a compact \(R\)-module. The only localizing subcategories of \(\D (K)\) are \(0\) and \(\D (K)\), so \(\ker ^{\dual }(F) = 0\).

Efimov uses a variant of this example to show that sequential colimits in \(\Pr ^{\dual }\) do not commute with kernels, so that \(\Pr ^{\dual }\) is not compactly assembled [Efimov (2024), Corollary 2.85].

We now describe pullbacks in \(\Pr ^{\dual }\). Let \(F\colon A \to C\) and \(G\colon B \to C\) be strongly continuous functors between dualizable categories. Their lax pullback \(L\) is the category of triples \((a,b,f)\) with \(a \in A\), \(b \in B\) and \(f\colon F(a) \to G(b)\). By adjunction, it is equivalent to the gluing of Construction 5.24 along the colimit-preserving functor \(F^RG\colon B \to A\). By Proposition 5.25, \(L\) is dualizable, and we have a semiorthogonal decomposition \(L = \lra {i_A(A),i_B(B)}\) with \(i_A(a) = (a,0,0)\) and \(i_B(b) = (0,b,0)\), and with projections \(\pi _A(a,b,f) = a\) and \(\pi _B(a,b,f) = b\). Consider the colimit-preserving functor \[ \Phi \colon L \to C, \qquad (a,b,f) \mapsto \cofib \big (f\colon F(a) \to G(b)\big ). \] Since \(\Phi i_A \simeq \Sigma F\) and \(\Phi i_B \simeq G\) are strongly continuous, \(\Phi \) is strongly continuous by Proposition 5.26(2). Its kernel \(\ker (\Phi )\) is the full subcategory of triples in which \(f\) is an equivalence, i.e. \(\ker (\Phi ) \simeq A \times _C B\) is the pullback in \(\PrL _{\st }\), which is computed in \(\widehat {\Cat }\).

Proposition 7.21 ([Efimov (2024), Proposition 2.86]). Let \(F\colon A \to C\) and \(G\colon B \to C\) be strongly continuous functors between dualizable categories, and let \(A \times _C^{\dual } B := \ker ^{\dual }(\Phi )\), with \(\Phi \) as above.

(1)

The category \(A \times ^{\dual }_C B\) is a pullback of \(F\) and \(G\) in \(\Pr ^{\dual }\). The functor \(A \times ^{\dual }_C B \to A \times _C B\) is fully faithful and strongly continuous, and its essential image is the largest full subcategory \(E \subseteq A \times _C B\) such that \(E\) is dualizable and the functors \(E \to A\) and \(E \to B\) are strongly continuous.

(2)

If \(G\) is a localization, then \(A \times ^{\dual }_C B \simeq A \times _C B\), and the projection \(A \times _C B \to A\) is a localization.

Proof.

(1) Let \(T\) be dualizable. By Proposition 7.19, \(\Map _{\Pr ^{\dual }}(T,A \times ^{\dual }_C B)\) is the union of the path components of \(\Map _{\Pr ^{\dual }}(T,L)\) consisting of functors \(H\) with \(\Phi H \simeq 0\), i.e. of strongly continuous functors \(T \to L\) landing in \(\ker (\Phi ) = A \times _C B\). By Proposition 5.26(1), a colimit-preserving functor \(H\colon T \to L\) is strongly continuous if and only if \(\pi _AH\) and \(\pi _BH\) are. So \(\Map _{\Pr ^{\dual }}(T,A \times ^{\dual }_C B)\) is the union of the path components of \(\Map _{\PrL _{\st }}(T,A \times _C B)\) consisting of functors whose two components are strongly continuous. As \(A \times _C B\) is a pullback in \(\PrL _{\st }\), this is \[ \Map _{\Pr ^{\dual }}(T,A) \times _{\Map _{\Pr ^{\dual }}(T,C)} \Map _{\Pr ^{\dual }}(T,B), \] naturally in \(T\). This proves that \(A \times ^{\dual }_C B\) is a pullback in \(\Pr ^{\dual }\).

Write \(k\colon A \times ^{\dual }_C B \hookrightarrow A \times _C B\) and \(j\colon A \times _C B \hookrightarrow L\) for the inclusions. Then \(jk\) is strongly continuous and \(j\) preserves colimits, so the right adjoint \(k^R \simeq (jk)^R j\) preserves colimits, i.e. \(k\) is strongly continuous. Finally, colimits in \(L\) are computed on the components \(a\) and \(b\), so a functor \(E \to L\) preserves colimits if and only if its composites with \(\pi _A\) and \(\pi _B\) do. Together with Proposition 5.26(1), this shows that for a full subcategory \(E \subseteq A \times _C B\), the functors \(E \to A\) and \(E \to B\) are strongly continuous if and only if the inclusion \(E \to L\) is. So the final assertion follows from the definition of \(\ker ^{\dual }(\Phi )\).

(2) Suppose that \(G^R\) is fully faithful. For \(c \in C\) and \((a,b,f) \in L\), we have \[ \Map _L\big ((a,b,f),(0,G^Rc,0)\big ) \simeq \fib \big (\Map _B(b,G^Rc) \to \Map _C(F(a),GG^Rc)\big ) \simeq \Map _C\big (\cofib (f),c\big ), \] using \(GG^R \simeq \id \). So \(\Phi \) has the right adjoint \(\Phi ^R(c) = (0,G^Rc,0)\), which preserves colimits and satisfies \(\Phi \Phi ^R \simeq GG^R \simeq \id \). Hence \(\Phi \) is a strongly continuous localization. By Lemma 2.13 and Proposition 3.19, its kernel \(\ker (\Phi ) = A \times _C B\) is dualizable and its inclusion into \(L\) is strongly continuous. Hence \(\ker ^{\dual }(\Phi ) = \ker (\Phi )\).

The functor \(R\colon A \to A \times _C B\), \(a \mapsto (a,G^RF(a),\theta )\), where \(\theta \colon F(a) \simeq GG^RF(a)\) is the inverse of the counit, is right adjoint to the projection: for \((a',b',\theta ') \in A \times _C B\), the anima of maps \((a',b',\theta ') \to R(a)\) is \[ \Map _A(a',a) \times _{\Map _C(F(a'),F(a))} \Map _B(b',G^RF(a)) \simeq \Map _A(a',a), \] since \(\Map _B(b',G^RF(a)) \simeq \Map _C(G(b'),F(a)) \simeq \Map _C(F(a'),F(a))\) via \(\theta '\). The composite of \(R\) with the projection is the identity, so \(R\) is fully faithful and the projection is a localization. □

In the talk, the functor \(A \times ^{\dual }_C B \to L\) was shown to be strongly continuous using the strong continuity of the projections \(\pi _A\) and \(\pi _B\); part (2) was not covered.

Corollary 7.22 ([Efimov (2024), Corollary 2.87]). A strongly continuous functor \(F\colon C \to D\) between dualizable categories is a monomorphism in \(\Pr ^{\dual }\) if and only if it is fully faithful.

Proof.

If \(F\) is fully faithful, then \(F \circ -\) is fully faithful on functor categories, so it induces monomorphisms on mapping spaces. Conversely, if \(F\) is a monomorphism, then the diagonal \(C \to C \times ^{\dual }_D C\) is an equivalence. Composing with the fully faithful functor \(C \times ^{\dual }_D C \to C \times _D C\) of Proposition 7.21(1), we find that the diagonal \(C \to C \times _D C\) is fully faithful. As in the proof of Proposition 7.3, it follows that \(\hom _C(x,y) \to \hom _D(Fx,Fy)\) is an equivalence for all \(x,y \in C\). □

Remark 7.23. A short exact sequence \(C \xrightarrow {i} D \xrightarrow {p} E\) of dualizable categories (Definition 7.8) is both a fiber sequence and a cofiber sequence in \(\Pr ^{\dual }\). It is a cofiber sequence since colimits in \(\Pr ^{\dual }\) are computed in \(\PrL _{\st }\) (Proposition 7.2). It is a fiber sequence since \(\ker ^{\dual }(p) = \ker (p) = C\), as the inclusion \(C \hookrightarrow D\) is strongly continuous; alternatively, apply Proposition 7.21(2) to the functors \(0 \to E\) and \(p\).
