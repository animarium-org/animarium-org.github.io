Proposition 2.25. Let \(A \xrightarrow {i} B \xrightarrow {p} C\) be a short exact sequence in \(\Cat _{\perf }\). Then \(i\) admits a right adjoint \(i^R\) if and only if \(p\) admits a right adjoint \(p^R\). In this case:

(1)

The functor \(p^R\) is fully faithful, with essential image the right orthogonal \(A^{\perp } = \{x \in B \mid \hom _B(ia,x) \simeq 0 \text { for all } a \in A\}\);

(2)

There is a semiorthogonal decomposition \(B = \lra {p^R(C),\, i(A)}\);

(3)

The sequence \(C \xrightarrow {p^R} B \xrightarrow {i^R} A\) is again short exact.

Proof.

Assume first that \(i^R\) exists, and for \(b \in B\) let \(L(b) := \cofib (ii^Rb \to b)\). As in the proof of Proposition 2.12(1), \(L(b) \in A^{\perp }\) and \(L\colon B \to A^{\perp }\) is left adjoint to the inclusion. Moreover, \(L(b) \simeq 0\) if and only if \(b \simeq ii^Rb\) lies in \(A\), so \(L\) inverts precisely the maps whose cofiber lies in \(A\). Since \(L\) is a reflective localization, it exhibits \(A^{\perp }\) as the localization of \(B\) at these maps [Lurie (2009), Proposition 5.2.7.12], so \(B/A \simeq A^{\perp }\). As \(A^{\perp }\) is closed under retracts in the idempotent complete category \(B\), it is idempotent complete, so \(C \simeq (B/A)^{\idem } \simeq A^{\perp }\), with \(p\) corresponding to \(L\). Hence \(p\) admits the fully faithful right adjoint \(A^{\perp } \hookrightarrow B\).

Conversely, assume that \(p^R\) exists. For \(a \in A\) and \(c \in C\) we have \(\hom _B(ia,p^Rc) \simeq \hom _C(pia,c) \simeq 0\), so \(p^R\) lands in \(A^{\perp }\). Every \(x \in A^{\perp }\) is local with respect to the maps \(s\colon b' \to b\) with \(\cofib (s) \in A\), i.e. \(s^*\colon \hom _B(b,x) \to \hom _B(b',x)\) is an equivalence. Hence \(\hom _{B/A}(qb,qx) \simeq \hom _B(b,x)\) for all \(b \in B\) and \(x \in A^{\perp }\) (for instance by Remark 2.8, applied to \(B\catop \)). Applying this to \(x = p^Rc\), we find that the composite \[ \hom _B(b,p^Rc) \to \hom _C(pb,pp^Rc) \to \hom _C(pb,c), \] whose second map is induced by the counit, is the adjunction equivalence. Its first map is an equivalence as well: since the functor \(B/A \to C\) induced by \(p\) is fully faithful, it is identified with the equivalence \(\hom _B(b,p^Rc) \simeq \hom _{B/A}(qb,qp^Rc)\). Hence so is the second map, i.e. the counit \(pp^Rc \to c\) induces an equivalence on \(\hom _C(pb,-)\) for all \(b \in B\). Since every object of \(C\) is a retract of an object of the form \(pb\), the counit is an equivalence, i.e. \(p^R\) is fully faithful. For \(b \in B\), let \(f \to b \to p^Rpb\) be the fiber sequence of the unit. Then \(p(f) \simeq 0\), so \(f\) lies in \(A\), and for \(a \in A\) we get \(\hom _B(ia,f) \simeq \hom _B(ia,b)\) since \(\hom _B(ia,p^Rpb) \simeq 0\). Hence \(b \mapsto f\) is right adjoint to \(i\).

It remains to prove (1)–(3). For (1), it remains to show that every \(x \in A^{\perp }\) lies in the image of \(p^R\). The fiber \(f\) of the unit \(x \to p^Rpx\) lies in \(A\), so \(\hom _B(f,x) \simeq 0\) and \(\hom _B(f,p^Rpx) \simeq 0\), whence \(\hom _B(f,f) \simeq 0\) and \(f \simeq 0\). For (2), we have \(\hom _B(ia,p^Rc) \simeq 0\), and every \(b \in B\) fits into the cofiber sequence \(ii^Rb \to b \to p^Rpb\). For (3), the kernel of \(i^R\) is \(A^{\perp } = p^R(C)\) by (1), and \(i^R\) is a coreflective localization since its left adjoint \(i\) is fully faithful; applying the first paragraph to opposite categories, \(i^R\) exhibits \(A\) as the Verdier quotient \(B/p^R(C)\). □
