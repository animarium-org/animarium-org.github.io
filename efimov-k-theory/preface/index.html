Efimov K-theory · Preface

Preface

These notes grew out of the research seminar on Efimov K-theory organized by Marc Hoyois at the University of Regensburg in the winter term 2026/27. They are organized by topic rather than by talk and include material from the workshop Dualizable categories and continuous K-theory (MPIM Bonn, September 2024) and from lectures by Dustin Clausen (July 2023).

I thank Marc Hoyois for organizing the seminar and all the speakers whose lectures contributed to these notes.

Generative AI has been used to convert lecture notes into LaTeX, reorganize the material, and draft additional exposition and proofs based on the literature. This includes material not covered in the lectures, as well as completions of arguments that were only sketched or omitted there. The source notes below distinguish lecture-based material from additions from the literature. Both kinds of material have received AI assistance. These remain working notes, and some of the added arguments have not yet been checked in detail.

Sources by chapter

Chapters 1–7 and Appendix A contain the material written so far. Chapters 8–11 are planned and are visibly marked as such. The following paragraphs record the sources of the written material. References for individual results and proofs also appear throughout the text.

Chapter 1

This chapter draws on Marc Hoyois’s first seminar talk (13 October 2026) and Dustin Clausen’s first three lectures (17–19 July 2023).

Chapter 2

Marc Hoyois’s first seminar talk (13 October 2026) contributes to Section 2.3, Section 2.6. Akhil Mathew’s first Bonn lecture (9 September 2024) is the source for Section 2.5 and parts of Section 2.2, Section 2.3, Section 2.4, Section 2.6.

Section 2.1 and most of Section 2.2, Section 2.3, Section 2.4 were added from the literature, using [Nikolaus and Scholze (2018); Calmès et al. (2020); Lurie (2009); Efimov (2024)]. The characterization of semiorthogonal decompositions in terms of adjoints (Proposition 2.23) comes from Jacopo Mazzei’s seminar talk (17 November 2026), supplemented by [Efimov (2024), Remark 2.75]. The proof of Tamme’s excision theorem (Theorem 2.41) was added from [Tamme (2018), Proposition 10 and Theorem 15], using lax pullbacks. The explanation of Theorem 2.40 was added from the stated non-finitary universal property of K-theory.

Chapter 3

This chapter is based on Marc Hoyois’s second seminar talk (20 October 2026). Akhil Mathew’s second Bonn lecture (9 September 2024) contributes to Section 3.2, Section 3.4. Dustin Clausen’s first two lectures (17–18 July 2023) contribute to Section 3.3, Section 3.4.

The introductory exposition was expanded with AI assistance. The example on continuous posets (Example 3.7) was added from the literature, following Johnstone–Joyal and Gierz et al. [Johnstone and Joyal (1982); Gierz et al. (2003)]. The proof of Proposition 3.22 was added using [Efimov (2024)]. The proof of Theorem 3.20 was revised using countable directed subposets, with the indexing lemma in Appendix A.

Chapter 4

This chapter is based on Jiapeng Liu’s seminar talk (3 November 2026). The converse implication in Proposition 4.9 and the proofs of Proposition 4.14, Corollary 4.12, Corollary 4.17, Lemma 4.20 were added using [Efimov (2024); Krause et al. (2024)]. A proof of (AB6) for sets and animae (Example 4.19) was added when preparing these notes. The interpolation results (Lemma 4.7, Proposition 4.8) were also added from the literature, following [Krause et al. (2024)]. Section 4.5 is based on Anton Zakrewski’s seminar talk (10 November 2026) and his notes for it, following [Efimov (2024), Section 2.8]; the second proof of Proposition 4.25 was added using compact exhaustions. The alternative compact-morphism definitions are collected in Remark 4.10 after the criteria establishing their agreement.

Chapter 5

Max Glaser’s seminar talk (27 October 2026), Dustin Clausen’s second lecture (18 July 2023), and Akhil Mathew’s third Bonn lecture (10 September 2024) contribute to Section 5.1, Section 5.2, Section 5.3. The proofs and references were supplemented from [Neeman (1992); Bhatt and Scholze (2017); Bhatt and Scholze (2022); Krause et al. (2024)]. The proofs of Proposition 5.2, Proposition 5.13 were added when preparing these notes. The latter supplies the Hilbert-space models for the compact-operator and \(S_{0+}\) kernels, including their symmetric monoidal structures; the identification of the latter with liquid rigidification is still quoted from Clausen.

The material on semiorthogonal decompositions and gluing in Section 5.4 comes from Jacopo Mazzei’s seminar talk (17 November 2026), following [Efimov (2024), Propositions 2.76--2.78]; parts of the proof of Proposition 5.26 were completed when preparing these notes. The Tate-extension material comes from Alexander Efimov’s fourth Bonn lecture (10 September 2024); the proofs of Proposition 5.28 and Example 5.30 were completed using [Efimov (2024); Hennion (2017); Calmès et al. (2020)].

Chapter 6

This chapter draws on Max Glaser’s seminar talk (27 October 2026) and Dustin Clausen’s second and third lectures (18–19 July 2023). Much of the treatment of rigidity and nuclearity was added from [Gaitsgory and Rozenblyum (2017); Ramzi (2024b); Efimov (2025); Scholze (2026); Andreychev (2023)]. The operator-algebra presentation and the Hochschild-homology obstruction in Example 6.21 were supplied from Efimov’s paper. The formula in Example 6.23 remains explicitly attributed to the unpublished lecture notes.

Chapter 7

Section 7.1, Section 7.2, Section 7.3 are based on Anton Zakrewski’s seminar talk (10 November 2026) and his notes for it. Section 7.4 is based on Jacopo Mazzei’s seminar talk (17 November 2026). Both follow [Efimov (2024), Sections 2.9--2.12], which was also used to complete the proofs. Proposition 7.3, Example 7.20, Corollary 7.22, Remark 7.17 and part (2) of Proposition 7.21 were added from [Efimov (2024)], and the proof of Proposition 7.9 uses [Ramzi (2024a), Proposition A.26]. The categorical facts preceding Proposition 7.14 use [Lurie (2017), Section 1.1.4]; the explanations of coherence, adjunction units and conservativity of the core were added when checking these proofs. Remark 7.6 identifies the continuous construction with the small Calkin construction in the compactly generated case, following [Efimov (2024), Proposition 2.59].
